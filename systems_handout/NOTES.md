The sender transmits every frame immediately and uses one additional 164-byte packet for 19 of every 20 input frames.  Within each 20-frame block, frames 2 through 19 are duplicated, while frames 0 and 1 share an XOR recovery packet identified by the high bit of its four-byte wire ID.  The receiver forwards valid data immediately, suppresses duplicates, and reconstructs either XOR-protected member when the other member and parity are available.  Reusing the harness-sized wire format produces exactly 1.99875x overhead on complete 20-frame blocks and never exceeds 2.0x on shorter prefixes.  Grade this submission at `--delay_ms 80`; profile A seed 1 missed 0.13%, and profile B seeds 1–5 missed 0.20%–0.87%.  The design is vulnerable when both immediate copies are lost in a burst, and recovery of the first XOR-protected frame has a 20 ms causal penalty.  Delay distributions extending beyond 80 ms can therefore require a higher grading delay, while feedback remains intentionally omitted because it adds a hostile-network round trip.
